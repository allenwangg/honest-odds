the hidden number

Every option price is a probability in disguise

Imagine a bet: I pay you $1 if SPY closes above $505 on Friday, nothing otherwise. What's it worth today? Obviously it depends on the odds — near-certain is worth almost a dollar, near-impossible almost nothing.

So the price is the probability. Drag the price and watch.

Price and probability are the same number wearing a different hat. And that contract already exists — buy the 505 call, sell the 506 call.

the whole distribution

Do it at every strike

One strike gives one probability. Every strike gives you the market's complete forecast for Friday's close — free, live, and almost nobody looks at it.

Formally: −∂C/∂K = Q(ST > K). Breeden–Litzenberger, 1978. Not exotic — plumbing.

the trade

Bet only on the disagreement

Pythia asks Claude for an independent estimate of the same probabilities — with one rule: the market's number is your prior, and you must argue before leaving it. Most of the time they agree and nothing happens. That's the correct outcome, and it's most of the day.

Position size scales with edge × confidence, hard-capped. Fractional Kelly under model uncertainty — maximize long-run growth without betting the account on being right about being right.

the unglamorous part

Eight gates, none of them intelligent

the trap

Stale quotes manufacture fake edges

Free market data goes stale, and stale prices invent opportunities that were never there. A naive agent "arbitrages" phantoms all day and never knows.

on any live chain
C − P = S − K

Put–call parity. Holds to within pennies on short-dated contracts. It's arbitrage, not a model.

when it breaks by dollars
the chain is lying

Pythia measures the median parity residual every cycle and refuses to trade any symbol that fails it.

the honest part

Every forecast gets graded

Each prediction is journaled and scored when it resolves — against reality, and against what the market believed at the same moment. The Brier score is a strictly proper scoring rule: the only way to score well is to report what you actually believe.
